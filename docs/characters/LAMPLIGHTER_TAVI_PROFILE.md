## Core Identity

Tavi is not a heroic lamplighter who loves the job.

He is a timid young man who took the lamp because Frey took the flag.

His light is ordinary. His fear is ordinary. His importance comes from whether he
can stand still when fear tells him to step back.

He should not be written as a joke coward. He is frightened, but he stayed.

That matters.

## The Lamp

Tavi's lamp is an ordinary patrol lantern.

Accepted details:

- It is not a holy relic.
- It is not ancient technology.
- It is used for night patrols and route checking.
- It needs oil.
- Its glass can get dirty.
- Wind and shaking hands can make the flame tremble.

The lamp is powerful only because someone carries it into fear.

## Childhood Mist Event

Tavi and Frey once ran outside as children to play and pretend to adventure. They
became lost in the hills when mist rolled in.

Tavi was terrified. He froze, cried, or could not move properly.

Frey comforted him. She was afraid too, but she forced herself to keep him from
breaking down.

Then Frey saw the patrol flag in the distance. The militia or patrol had come to
find them. The flag led them home.

The same event shaped them differently:

- Frey saw a flag and found a lifelong belief.
- Tavi saw the same fog and kept the fear.

He remembers being saved, but he also remembers being helpless.

## Why He Became Lamplighter

Tavi did not become lamplighter because he wanted a calling.

He became lamplighter because Frey wanted to hold the flag.

When they were young, Frey looked at the flag and wanted to become the person who
could call others home. Tavi did not have that courage or that brightness. The
only related role he could imagine was standing beside the flag with a lamp.

His childish, sincere thought:

> If she holds the flag, I can at least hold the light beside her.

This is the emotional foundation of his entire route.

## Personality

Tavi is:

- Timid.
- Soft-spoken.
- Easily frightened.
- Sincere.
- Loyal.
- More observant than he appears.
- Capable of care, but slow to act under direct danger.

He may apologize too often. He may try to help through small practical acts:
checking oil, cleaning glass, fixing a hinge, staying near the gate even if he
does not dare to walk far.

He is not useless. He is afraid.

## Relationship With Frey

Tavi and Frey are childhood friends.

Their bond is mostly dependence, with a small unspoken romantic shade that has
never grown into confession or formal romance.

Frey moves forward. Tavi follows.

Frey speaks loudly when fear rises. Tavi becomes quieter.

Frey props up his courage. Tavi quietly tries to support her by carrying the
lamp, checking the path, or remaining close.

The tragedy is that he has relied on her courage for too long. He loves or needs
her in a way he has not named, but because he never names it, Frey keeps standing
in front alone.

## Relationship With The Protagonist

At first, Tavi may see the protagonist as someone who can do what he cannot:
leave the gate, face monsters, and return.

He may be grateful, intimidated, or ashamed around the protagonist.

In the second run, the protagonist becomes the person who helps Tavi say what he
has avoided saying to Frey.

The protagonist does not need to turn him into a warrior. The protagonist helps
him become able to stand in his own role.

## First Run

Accepted direction: Tavi fails at the critical moment in the first run.

This failure should happen during a major route crisis, boss-related event, or
fog attack where the danger is real.

First-run chain:

1. A position must be lit, held, or reached.
2. Tavi understands that this is his responsibility.
3. He sees the danger clearly.
4. He freezes.
5. He wants to move, but his body does not obey.
6. Frey steps into the gap.
7. Frey raises the flag and dies holding the direction for the protagonist or
   others.
8. Tavi survives and is devastated.

He did not choose Frey's death. He did not run because he did not care.

He froze because death was suddenly real.

## First-Run Aftermath

After Frey dies, Tavi becomes able to perform her role more reliably.

This should not read as simple growth.

He is not suddenly brave. The pain has numbed the fear.

The player may see him become quieter, steadier, and more competent with the
lamp. At first this can seem like maturity. Later the emotional reading should
become darker:

- His fear did not disappear.
- Something in him went dead around it.
- He finally understands what his lamp meant, but too late.

He may continue lighting lamps because stopping would make Frey's death mean
nothing.

## Second Run

Accepted direction: Tavi's second-run side route is the condition that can save
Frey.

Second-run knowledge can reveal where the attack or route crisis will happen,
but preventing the tragedy requires Tavi's emotional change.

Second-run route shape:

1. The player carries future knowledge that a crisis is coming.
2. The player engages Tavi before the event.
3. Tavi is forced to face why he became lamplighter.
4. He admits his dependence on Frey.
5. He says, directly or indirectly, that he never wanted to stand behind while
   she carried everything.
6. When the crisis comes, Frey prepares to sacrifice herself as in the first run.
7. Tavi acts first.
8. He is still terrified, but he holds the lamp in the dangerous place.
9. Frey no longer needs to die.

This route saves both characters in different ways.

## Core Confession Or Admission

Tavi's second-run breakthrough does not need to be a romantic confession.

It should be a plain, awkward admission of dependence and love-like loyalty.

Possible emotional shape:

> I did not become the lamplighter because I was brave. I only wanted to stand
> where your flag could see my light.

Or even simpler:

> I was never trying to guard the road. I was trying not to let you stand there
> alone.

The exact runtime line should be rewritten later in the game's prose style, but
the meaning should stay.

## Tavi's Growth

Tavi does not become fearless.

His real growth is:

- He admits fear.
- He admits dependence.
- He admits Frey has been carrying too much alone.
- He acts while still afraid.

This is important. If he becomes a sudden hero, the route loses its human weight.

## Frey's Growth Through Tavi

Tavi's second-run action changes Frey.

When Tavi stands in danger with the lamp, Frey experiences what the elder has
carried for years: the terror of watching someone important step toward death.

This teaches Frey:

- Fear is not always cowardice.
- Entrusting a life-or-death task to someone important is terrifying.
- Permanent loss is a wound, not a lesson that disappears.
- A flag alone is not enough. A road home needs someone who lights it too.

This makes Frey's rescue route mutual, not one-sided.

## Fate Direction

First run:

- Tavi survives.
- Frey dies.
- Tavi becomes emotionally hollowed and more functional only because grief
  numbs him.

Second run:

- Tavi can grow before Frey's death.
- Frey can be saved.
- Tavi becomes a true lamplighter without becoming a fearless warrior.

## Entrusted Flame

Tavi's entrusted flame is the ordinary lamp carried by a frightened person who
chooses not to step back.

Potential relics, not yet approved:

- Cracked lantern glass.
- A soot-darkened wick case.
- A patrol lantern with Frey's flag thread tied to the handle.

If Frey is saved, Tavi's relic may not be a death relic. It may become a shared
route symbol with Frey's patrol flag.

## Dialogue Voice

Tavi should sound:

- Hesitant.
- Honest when cornered.
- Gentle.
- Nervous.
- More practical than poetic.

He should not constantly self-mock. His fear is painful, not comic relief.

Good line shape:

- A small practical detail about oil, glass, wind, or the path.
- A pause or apology.
- A quiet admission that he is afraid.

Avoid:

- Turning him into a joke coward.
- Making him suddenly fearless after one conversation.
- Giving the lamp supernatural power without approval.
- Making his affection for Frey loud or melodramatic too early.
